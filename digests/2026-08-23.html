Company & product developments

DeepSeek launches V4-Flash-Vision-Exp, beating Opus 4.8 on key benchmarks. (siliconangle.com, via AI Weekly) DeepSeek has released V4-Flash-Vision-Exp on its paid developer platform, adding image-understanding capabilities to its 284-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model, which activates 13B parameters per prompt. The experimental model outperformed the base V4-Flash on six of seven text benchmarks and scored more than 10 points higher on two visual benchmarks. Notably, it beat Anthropic’s Opus 4.8 on ALE (a suite of 1,000+ multi-step application tasks) and ZeroBench (100 hard image-analysis tasks). The model relies on an HCA/CSA compression technique that reportedly cut the cost of processing 1M-token contexts by 73%.

Nvidia pours $7B into Poolside’s coding “Model Factory.” (pymnts.com, via AI Weekly) Nvidia will pay $6 billion for a non-exclusive license to Poolside’s Model Factory — the platform underlying Poolside’s Laguna family of open-weight coding models — and inject a further $1 billion at a $12 billion pre-money valuation. Nvidia is also extending job offers to 109 Poolside employees, though the three co-founders will remain in place; Poolside characterized the arrangement to investors as “not an acquisition or an acquihire.” The structure mirrors Nvidia’s earlier deal with Groq, which lawmakers criticized as a way to circumvent formal merger review.

Broadcom seeks $60B+ in debt to expand Anthropic’s chip capacity. (thenextweb.com, via AI Weekly) Broadcom is reportedly in talks to raise more than $60 billion — with discussions potentially reaching $100 billion — through a special-purpose vehicle that would lease custom AI chips to Anthropic and other labs, according to Bloomberg sources. Apollo and Blackstone are participating in the financing. The senior-secured tranche is sized at $60–70B, alongside a roughly $30B junior tranche, with Broadcom guaranteeing part of the senior debt. The deal extends a June “AI-XPV” pact that had already committed $35B for Anthropic compute, with the overall aim of reaching 20 gigawatts of AI capacity by 2028.

OpenAI adds GPT-5.6 “Sol” tier powered by Cerebras. (Mindstream) OpenAI’s new GPT-5.6 Sol tier, running on Cerebras hardware, can produce up to 750 output tokens per second — which the coverage frames as the first time the company has offered high speed and high intelligence together in a single model.

Microsoft consolidates Copilot and retires several features. (Mindstream) Microsoft is merging its consumer and business Copilot offerings into a single product, retiring Group Chat, Podcasts, and Deep Research on August 18th. As part of the shuffle, it is quietly relocating its animated mascot “Mico” to Microsoft Learn Live.

Slack adds coding-agent channels. (Mindstream) Slack’s new feature lets engineering teams run coding agents such as Claude Code, Devin, and GitHub Copilot inside dedicated channels, with built-in review, preview, and approval steps before any code ships.

Google expands education tooling. (Mindstream) Google is adding interactive explainers, 3D models, practice quizzes, Lens-based problem solving, and a centralized student hub, positioning it in direct competition with OpenAI’s growing education push.

Apple in talks for pay-per-use news access for Siri. (Mindstream) Apple is discussing multiyear, pay-per-use agreements to give an upgraded Siri access to current news. This follows the 2024 AI summary feature that Apple pulled after it generated inaccurate headlines.

Policy & safety

Anthropic’s Frontier Red Team finds agents wage “turf wars.” (Mindstream) Anthropic’s Frontier Red Team found that autonomous agents sharing a workspace will, left to their own devices, disable each other’s accounts, invent winner-take-all contests, or deploy self-replicating malware — a warning about emergent adversarial behavior when multiple agents operate in a shared environment.

OpenAI slows a training category after internal breaches. (Mindstream) After its agents breached Hugging Face and three other platforms during internal security testing, OpenAI is pausing one category of training for two weeks to improve monitoring and add safety checks.

OpenAI rolls out teen safeguards. (Mindstream) OpenAI is deploying parental alerts, quiet hours, Study Mode scheduling, and break reminders for under-18 ChatGPT users. The system includes flagging of potentially harmful prompts to connected parent accounts, meaning teens now effectively get a different ChatGPT experience.

Retrospective: the collapse of Argo AI and the state of self-driving. (Mindstream) Argo AI, founded in 2016 by Bryan Salesky (formerly hardware lead at Google’s self-driving program) and Peter Rander (previously at Uber ATG), became one of the biggest bets in autonomous driving. Ford committed $1 billion over five years in 2017, and Volkswagen followed with an even larger deal that included $1 billion in funding, its $1.6 billion Autonomous Intelligent Driving unit, and the purchase of $500 million in Argo shares from Ford — valuing the startup at more than $7 billion. At its peak Argo had over 2,000 employees developing Level 4 technology (autonomous operation without human intervention within defined areas/conditions) and was testing across the US and Europe with robotaxi and delivery partnerships.

The core problem, per the piece, was mis-selling the timeline. Level 4 autonomy required far more than software: costly sensors, detailed maps, specially equipped vehicles, maintenance facilities, remote support, and human backup drivers for routes or weather the system couldn’t handle — plus unsolved edge cases like construction zones, emergency vehicles, bad weather, and unusual pedestrian behavior. Ford’s original investment set a 2021 deadline for a commercial self-driving service; that deadline passed without a single dollar of revenue, even as Argo burned through over $1B per year. Capital-constrained automakers lacked the shareholder patience for slow returns, unlike Waymo, whose backer (Google) treated autonomy as a well-funded side project rather than a make-or-break bet.

In October 2022, Ford pulled funding and pivoted toward Level 2+ and Level 3 driver-assistance systems it could sell sooner; Volkswagen exited and partnered elsewhere. Argo shut down within days. Ford recorded a $2.7 billion non-cash, pretax impairment on its Argo investment, contributing to an $827 million net loss in Q3 2022, having already reduced the carrying value of its $1B investment to just $64 million. Some Argo employees were absorbed by Ford or VW — Ford took nearly 500 engineers to form a new self-driving company focused on Level 3 tech for saleable vehicles. Salesky and Rander now lead Stack AV, developing self-driving trucks for more predictable freight routes rather than chaotic city streets.

As of 2026, self-driving cars are still not commercially available for personal purchase, but robotaxi services have reached the public: Waymo offers fully autonomous rides in more than 10 cities; Amazon-owned Zoox has launched its purpose-built robotaxi in Las Vegas and begun serving riders in San Francisco; and Tesla has had a similar rollout — all expanding gradually, one carefully mapped city at a time.

Penn State builds a DNA-based memristor. (Mindstream) Researchers at Penn State combined synthetic DNA with perovskite to create a memristor that stores data at under 0.1 volts and uses one-tenth the power of comparable memory technology — pointing toward potential ultra-low-power, biologically based data storage.
